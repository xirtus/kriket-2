Quick Start

Launch the game, choose a format and conditions, then play from the first ball. KRIKET supports direct play and AI-vs-AI broadcast mode.

First Five Minutes

  1. Open the game and choose PLAY or WATCH AI v AI.
  2. Select format, team, opponent, difficulty, pitch, weather and time of day.
  3. Bat with aim plus shot keys, or bowl with delivery type plus target marker.
  4. Use R to run and F to send the batters back.
  5. Use Enter to skip between-ball pauses and Esc to pause.

Match Setup

The setup screen defines the full playing context: match length, teams, venue behavior, light, weather and how forgiving the human-facing systems should be.

Formats

T20 compresses pressure into twenty overs. ODI stretches innings management and bowler rotation. Test uses four innings and rewards patience, wear and conditions.

Difficulty

Rookie expands batting contact windows and slows AI reactions. Club is the intended tuning. Pro narrows batting windows and sharpens AI execution.

Watch Mode

AI-vs-AI mode runs the cricket engine as a broadcast. Speed keys 1, 2 and 3 switch live, 2x and 4x playback.

Simulation Model

The game is built around real-world units and live physics: meters, kilograms, seconds, regulation ball dimensions, pitch dimensions and procedural player rigs.

What Is Actually Simulated

  • Quadratic drag, Magnus lift, seam axis, spin and swing.
  • Conventional swing that fades with shine, plus reverse swing with wear and pace.
  • Pitch restitution, friction, seam grip, variable bounce and rolling resistance.
  • Bat-ball collision with contact quality, edges, misses and mistimed chips.
  • Fielding reaction, pursuit, catch probability, dives, throws and overthrows.
  • Dismissals including bowled, caught, LBW, run out and stumped.

Real Dimensions

The constants use SI units: 9.81 m/s gravity, 20.12 m pitch length, 0.7112 m stumps, 0.965 m bat length and a regulation 0.156 kg ball.

Characters

Cricketers are procedural capsule/sphere rigs with helmets, caps, pads, bat, gait animation, batting stance, bowling action, keeper crouch and celebration poses.

AI

Bowler brains choose plans by phase and conditions. Batter brains manage shot choice, risk and resource pressure. Captaincy adjusts fields based on scoring patterns.

Strategy Guide

Good KRIKET play comes from reading line, length, phase and risk. The ball obeys physics, so the best response depends on what the delivery is actually doing.

Presentation

The game aims for broadcast cricket: readable score state, visible ball physics, lively stadium context and camera decisions that follow match events.

HUD

The scorebug shows team, score, wickets, overs, current rate and over progress. The strip shows batter and bowler lines. Recent-ball bubbles help read momentum.

Cameras

The director supports TV framing, ball-follow, wicket zoom, side-on replay and behind-batter perspectives. QA hooks can freeze and move the camera for exact screenshots.

World

The stadium includes crowd color, advertisements, boundary rope, pitch texture, footmarks, cracks, sky presets and day, twilight or night lighting with floodlights.

Glossary

Seam presentation: the ball's seam angle as it hits air and pitch, influencing swing and deviation.

Magnus lift: aerodynamic force from spin, used for dip, drift and turn behavior.

Variable bounce: surface randomness that produces shooters or the odd ball that rears.

Contact quality: a 0-to-1 signal for how well the bat met the delivery.

Free hit: a no-ball consequence where some dismissal types are disallowed.

Field preset: a tactical placement set chosen by the AI captain for phase and batter behavior.

Wagon wheel: a future shot map showing scoring direction and distance.

Beehive: a delivery map showing where balls pass the batter or stumps.
